Preface · Orthogonal

The same five stages, in a crystal.

Before gospel, before SGD — the shape already existed in geometric form. A massive, unreadable 3D volume is isolated along a specific plane of incidence. A live kinetic event strikes it, scattering across active vector paths, which finally land and lock into fixed coordinates on a film. But a trained eye doesn't just look at a single point; they read the entire network of points back into the original 3D law.

I · Landscape

The corpus a lifetime is trained on.

Not a genre — a loss surface. Andraé Crouch, the Winans, BeBe & CeCe, Fred Hammond, Commissioned, Mary Mary: decades of Sunday mornings, theological weight, collective memory, laid down before anyone touches a key. A childhood in the house, not a tourist's visit to the tradition.

This is the terrain every chord gets voiced against. It never appears in a transcript. It's the reason the transcript needed to exist — and the reason it will always undersell what actually happened.

II · UB — the Room

Raw, noisy, unnotatable signal.

The stamping feet. The reframe. The sudden, unpredictable "Amen!" from the back pew. Full of measurement error, by design — you can't fully notate the kinetic energy of three hundred people moving together.

This is the input nobody wrote down, and the one every real chord was actually responding to. Music built in real time by the congregation's answer, not composed in advance.

III · SGD

The live, sub-second optimization loop.

The preacher changes cadence. The room erupts or falls silent. The organist re-voices the chord — the dim7 leaning into the dominant, the sus4 held a beat too long — before there's time to think. That loop repeats continuously, driving the room toward its maximum, and it only runs where stage II is happening: live, with stakes.

Berklee gives the grammar to name the chord it produces. Neither grammar nor a keyboard gives you the thousands of live steps that trained the hand to do it in under a second. That's not a knowledge gap — it's a reps gap, and it closes only here.

IV · UI/UX

What stage III leaves behind.

The chord. The take. The polished page with the elegant typography. This is the fossil — the only part of the loop that survives it, and the only part a page, a recording, or an HTML file can actually hold.

Okubala si Kulaba — the setlist is the tensor; the notes below are the Jacobian. True of the loop that made them. Not true of a page that only counts them.

A fossil is not nothing. It's the necessary residue of stage III — but by itself it's still just counting. What happens to it next is the whole point of stage V.

V · Ecosystem

Siyabonana — reading the fossil back into the loop.

When BeBe, CeCe, and Mary Mary trade a knowing smile on stage, they're reading the stage-IV fossil and reconstructing the stage-III loop that produced it. Not "nice chord." I know exactly why you went there. The artifact is never the message. The trajectory is.
